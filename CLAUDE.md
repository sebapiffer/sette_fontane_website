# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

One-page showcase site for **Sette Fontane**, an organic winery in Giovo (Val di Cembra, Trentino). First wine: San Florian, a Müller Thurgau in two formats (Renana 0.75 L, Magnum 1.5 L). The original spec is `instructions.md`; brand source assets (logo PDF, label, bottle photos) live in `references/`. UI copy and comments are in Italian.

## Commands

```bash
npm run dev       # Vite dev server
npm run build     # production build to dist/
npm run preview   # serve the production build
```

There is no lint or test tooling configured.

## Architecture

Vite + React 18 + Tailwind v3, animations via GSAP (ScrollTrigger + Flip) through the `useGSAP` hook from `@gsap/react`. Icons from `lucide-react`.

- `src/App.jsx` — registers GSAP plugins once and stacks the page sections in order: Preloader, Navbar, Hero, Azienda, ChiSiamo, Territorio, IntroVini, SanFlorian, ComingSoon, Footer. It also mounts the two entry popups (`VerificaEta`, `BannerCookie`) outside the `Routes`, so they survive route changes, and — when the initial route isn't `/`, i.e. no Preloader — signals ready itself with `document.fonts.ready.then(attendiEta).then(segnalaPronto)`: the age gate holds `sf:ready` on subpages exactly as it does on the Home.
- `src/components/IntroVini.jsx` — the "La nostra cantina" separator (`id: la-nostra-cantina`; no nav link points at it — the menu jumps straight to `#san-florian`): a full-bleed band, long and low, in solid `antracite`. Its whole job is the cut, so it breaks every habit of the other sections on purpose — no photographic background, no scrim, no columns, no CTA, no `SfondoSezione`. It was `moro` until the client read that band as "too brown and not cohesive", and the diagnosis stuck: the home's only grounds are `antracite` and `creta`/`offwhite` with `tortora` as an accent, so `moro` — a ground used nowhere else — read as foreign rather than as a deliberate caesura. On `antracite` the band is now the **opening of the dark chapter** instead of a stripe of its own: the top edge still cuts hard against Territorio's offwhite, but the ground runs on into San Florian, so the colour alone no longer closes it. The bottom cut is made by `.vini-chiusura`, a full-bleed tortora hairline — the same accent as the hero's rule under the title. It must **not** be given the `.vini-filo` class: the Viticcio anchors its crossing nodes to those elements *by index*, and a third one would shift them. The drops logo sits *inside* the title, between "La nostra" and "cantina" (`vini.titleParts` in `content.js`): it's the band's only vertical element and it sets the height. It is **perfectly centred on the viewport**, and that is structural, not tuned: the two title halves are each `flex-1 basis-0`, so they measure the same regardless of the tronconi being different widths ("LA NOSTRA" > "CANTINA") — the text hugs the logo, the hairline takes the remainder out to the edge. Don't "fix" the centring by nudging margins; if it drifts, a half has stopped being `basis-0`. Below `md` the size, tracking and gap all step down (`tracking-[0.16em] md:tracking-[0.28em]`, and the `-mr` trim tracks it): at 360 px the full title doesn't fit, and what gets squeezed out is the hairlines — they collapsed to **width 0**, taking the Viticcio's channel with them. Those numbers were measured across 360/390/430/768/1024/1440/1920 with a Playwright scratch script, not eyeballed; the left hairline (the mobile anchor) bottoms out at ~14 px (it was ~17 until `DropsLogo` was redrawn on the PDF geometry, which is wider at the same height — any further widening of the logo eats this margin directly). The title is `font-bold` — Mason Sans ships a real 700 face (`MasonSans-Bold.woff2`), so this is not synthetic. Verify weight by **measuring**, never by looking at a downscaled screenshot: a full-width capture shrunk to view thins the strokes enough to look like 400. The reliable check is that the rendered word's width matches a forced-`700` span at the same size and tracking (at 46.4 px, "La" is 81.98 px at 700 vs 79.98 at 400) — an hour was lost chasing a bold that was there all along. Any further growth of the title or logo has to be re-measured against that floor; the drops fall in with `CADUTA_GOCCE`, the same cadence as the preloader and hero. The visible title is `aria-hidden` — split in two around a logo it doesn't read as a sentence — and an `sr-only` h2 carries the real text. The two hairlines are `.vini-filo`, and they are **load-bearing for the Viticcio**: each one starts exactly where the title ends and runs to the edge, i.e. its rect *is* the free channel beside the text, which is why the tendril's crossing nodes anchor to `.vini-filo` (index 1 = right, desktop; index 0 = left, mobile) instead of to fractions of the section — the channel then tracks the title's width by itself at any viewport, no retuning.
- `src/components/Preloader.jsx` — full-screen loading overlay with the drops logo looping (same fall-in cadence as the hero opening). It waits for window `load` + `document.fonts.ready` (min 1.6 s, max 6 s), fades out, unmounts, then sets `document.documentElement.dataset.ready` and dispatches `sf:ready` and a `ScrollTrigger.refresh()`. **It emits the two opening signals, and they are deliberately two.** `sf:caricato` fires when the fade finishes: loading is over, and the age gate — which sits *under* the preloader — comes on stage. `sf:ready` fires only once the visitor has actually answered (`attendiEta`), and that's what the hero opening, Lenis and the reveals wait on: announcing both together would play the hero's entrance behind a closed overlay, and whoever came in would find it already over. The 6 s cap covers a load that never finishes, not a visitor who hasn't answered. The scroll lock also goes through `bloccaScroll('preloader')` rather than writing `body.overflow` directly, because the age gate holds a lock at the same time — see `src/lib/bloccoScroll.js`.
- `src/components/VerificaEta.jsx` — age gate, the site's entry threshold: a full-screen `role="dialog" aria-modal` overlay. **It sits one floor *below* the preloader (`z-[95]` against z-100), not above, and that is the whole trick**: the overlay is opaque from the first frame, so none of the site is ever visible, but what plays on top of it is the drops loading. When the preloader is done it fades — and since both share the antracite ground it doesn't uncover the page, it uncovers this screen: the logo leaves and the threshold composes in its place. The content inside waits for `sf:caricato` before mounting, or it would assemble behind the drops while they're still looping. "No" swaps in a courtesy screen **in place** — no external redirect, ever — with a discreet way back for a mis-click. The confirmation goes to `localStorage` (see `consensi.js`), so it's asked once per browser, not once per tab. Three details are load-bearing. Focus is taken **at the end of the entrance timeline**, not at mount, because until then the rows are at `autoAlpha: 0`, i.e. `visibility: hidden`, and `focus()` on an invisible element silently does nothing (the focus stays on `<body>` — measured, not guessed); the Tab trap listens on the *document* in capture, both because focus may still be outside and so it can `progress(1)` the entrance and hand the keyboard the buttons at once instead of making it wait the animation out; and ESC is deliberately not handled, since answering is the only way out. Two layout rules come from the same measuring session: the rows rise **while** the drops are still falling (`'-=1.2'`, not after them) because end-to-end — preloader loop + fade + entrance — the buttons were only usable at 4.3 s, and the vertical rhythm is in `clamp(…vh…)` with `my-auto` centring rather than fixed margins and `justify-center`, because a centred flex whose content overflows clips its own **top** with no way to scroll back to it (verified at 900×520, 844×390, 360×640 — the drops logo was disappearing past the edge).
- `src/components/BannerCookie.jsx` — cookie notice, at `z-[55]` (above the navbar, below the San Florian zoom and the page-transition curtain). A **statement, not a choice**: the site sets no cookies at all — no analytics, no pixels, no third-party embeds (the map is a local image, the video is in `public/`) — so there is nothing to consent to and nothing to switch off; a "reject" toggle that disables nothing would be theatre. One button, plus the note about what actually stays on the device, which is what keeps the claim honest. It waits for `sf:ready` (behind the preloader *and* the age gate it would otherwise appear under an opaque overlay) and never locks scrolling — the page is readable with it open, which is the point.
- `src/lib/ambiente.js` — shared page-context contracts: reduced-motion, the md/lg breakpoints, and the **two opening signals**, both built by the same `creaSegnale` factory (flag on `<html>` + event, fire once, and `quando…` runs immediately if the moment has already passed so nobody waits forever for a signal sent before they were listening). `sf:caricato` = the preloader is done and has uncovered the age gate; `sf:ready` = the visitor is actually in, and everything with a mount-time entrance hangs off *that* one. On subpages, where there is no Preloader, `App.jsx` emits `sf:caricato` itself at mount.
- `src/lib/consensi.js` — the two entry acknowledgements in one place: storage keys, `try/catch` accessors (Safari private mode throws on `localStorage` — the site then just behaves like a first visit instead of crashing), and `attendiEta()`, the promise the rest of the site waits on before raising the curtain. A promise and not an event, so whoever asks *after* the confirmation finds it already resolved — the same rule as `quandoPronto`.
- `src/lib/bloccoScroll.js` — keyed scroll lock. Preloader and age gate cover the page at the same time and both want it frozen; with each one writing `body.overflow` directly, the first to finish would unlock for the other (the preloader finishes loading while the gate is still up, and the page would scroll under the overlay). Each takes and releases its own key, and the page unlocks when none are left. It also stops/starts Lenis, which drives `window.scrollTo` itself and wouldn't be held by `overflow: hidden` alone.
- `src/data/content.js` — **all copy lives here**, never hardcoded in components. Texts marked `[placeholder]` await final copy from the client; the "L'azienda" copy is real (taken from the packaging carton). Keep the structure intact when editing. The home's ChiSiamo cards deliberately carry **no `bio`**: the two sentences that were there were invented placeholders, and once the real roles arrived they said the opposite of them. `ChiSiamo.jsx` renders the bio only if present, so putting the key back is enough to bring it back. A person's `role` (home) and `ruolo` (`chiSiamoPage`) are the same label and must be **changed together** — the same brother cannot be introduced two different ways on two pages.
- `src/hooks/useReveal.js` — shared scroll-reveal used by every section: elements tagged `data-reveal` get one of **four distinct text entrances** (see the `VARIANTI` vocabulary below), `data-reveal-words` (SplitHeading) rise word by word out of their masks, `data-reveal-img` uncover via clip-path. Wrapped in `gsap.matchMedia` so `prefers-reduced-motion` disables it. New sections should reuse this hook rather than writing their own reveal. The entrance is **scrubbed**, not time-based: each element gets its own ScrollTrigger and advances as it crosses the window (`top 90%` → `top 56%`, scrub 0.6 — a soft follow, because Lenis drives the page and a rigid scrub would hand back the wheel's micro-jitter), so the word stagger becomes spatial and scrolling back retracts the text. Two things are load-bearing there: the ranges are wrapped in `clamp()`, or elements in the last viewport (the whole footer) would never reach the end of their range and would sit at opacity 0 forever; and elements **already above the fold at mount** are excluded from the scrub entirely (measured once with `getBoundingClientRect` — scroll is 0 at that moment on both Home and subpages) and keep the old one-shot staggered entrance, because clamping their start back to scroll 0 would leave a page header waiting for a scroll that hasn't happened. That one-shot timeline is paused and plays on `sf:ready`, same rule as the hero. The line split (modelled on kettmeir.com, the client's reference) uses `SplitText.create(..., {type: 'lines', autoSplit: true})`: `autoSplit` re-splits when the fonts land and at every width change — line breaks depend on the viewport, so a split frozen at mount is wrong after the first resize — and `onSplit` returns the animation so GSAP rebuilds it with the lines. The above-fold splits hand their tween to the opening timeline rather than firing it: a re-split after the opening has finished then lands on a timeline already at its end and renders straight to the final state instead of replaying the entrance. Lines are deliberately **not** masked (`mask: 'lines'` is available and unused): the site's text runs from `leading-relaxed` prose down to tight eyebrows, and a mask flush to the line box shaves descenders and accents — the same problem `SplitHeading` solves by hand with per-case padding. Only prose is split; `[data-reveal]` containers that lay out something else (the footer's logo/social blocks, a div around a CTA) stay block fades, and `data-reveal="blocco"` opts a paragraph out. The site's one text gesture is **`inchiostro`**: words ink in one after another, as if the page were being written. It does **not** move — the emphasis is in how the words arrive: each starts fully **transparent** and opacifies (ink taking on the paper), and **tortora**, easing to its own colour, so the ink is literally the brand accent settling into the text (tortora reads on both antracite and creta, so no word ever vanishes mid-transition); the wave is long (`passo: 0.085`) so the writing is seen crossing the paragraph. The entrance used to *also* start blurred (`blur(9px)` → 0) — the prettier gesture, but the least legible one: for the first half of the run the word was unreadable, and since the run is scrubbed that half lasts as long as the reader's scroll, so a paragraph could be sat in half out of focus. Transparency alone keeps the letterforms sharp the whole way, so a half-arrived word still reads. Removing it also removes the compositing cost: an inline `filter`, even `blur(0px)`, promoted *every word* to its own layer, which is why `pulisci` had to clear it; `pulisci: 'color'` now only has to keep an inline colour from permanently outranking the element's Tailwind classes. `VARIANTI` also holds `righe` (lines rise + fade), `tendina` (lines wiped left→right) and `lettere` (chars cascade); nothing assigns them automatically any more, they are opt-in via `data-reveal="<nome>"`. Titles keep their own gesture (SplitHeading's masked word-rise, the site's signature) and vary only in *choreography*, `stagger.from` alternating `start`/`center` by section. What gets split is decided by `bersagli()`: a **text run** (a text element with no block children) is split directly; a `[data-reveal]` that is a *container* has its text runs split instead — one `SplitText`, one tween, so the stagger flows through the whole block rather than restarting at each paragraph. That second path is what covers ChiSiamo's `<figcaption>` (name + role, and the optional bio). A container holding anything non-textual (`img, svg, video, button, .btn` — the footer's logo and social icons, a div wrapping a CTA) keeps the plain block fade, since those parts cannot be animated word by word. Since the Preloader — the only emitter of `sf:ready` — lives on the Home alone, `App.jsx` now signals ready itself (after `document.fonts.ready`) when the initial route isn't `/`; before that, landing straight on a subpage left Lenis and the Viticcio waiting on a signal that never came.
- `src/components/Cursore.jsx` — custom cursor, modelled on kettmeir.com: a 12 px tortora dot that lags the pointer (0.42 s) and **deforms with velocity** — stretched along the direction of travel, squashed across it, like a falling drop (the brand mark, drawn by the motion instead of by a path). Two transform levels on purpose: the wrapper is a 0×0 point carrying position, the circle inside carries only rotation and squash, so the per-frame velocity write and the state tweens never fight over one matrix. Velocity is measured on the *rendered* position (`gsap.getProperty`), not on mouse events, so the deformation dies out by itself as the dot catches up. Over anything interactive the filled dot opens into a 34 px ring — animated as **width/height, not `scale`**: scaling a 1px border thickens it, and compensating with `borderWidth: 1/scale` makes the browser round it to 0 and the ring disappears entirely (measured). The system arrow is **not** hidden (the reference doesn't hide it either): the dot is an accent that follows, so pointing stays precise and neither a JS failure nor a touch device can leave the page with no pointer. Tortora is the one accent that reads on both the antracite and creta grounds, so unlike the Navbar it does not follow the per-section theme. Mounts only on `(pointer: fine)` without `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- `src/components/SfondoSezione.jsx` — full-bleed photographic section background (Azienda, ChiSiamo, Territorio, SanFlorian, Footer; ComingSoon and IntroVini are deliberately left clean — ComingSoon is the pause before the footer and the end of the Viticcio, IntroVini is a flat colour cut and a photo would soften exactly the edge it exists to make). Decorative only (`aria-hidden`, empty `alt` — the informative photos stay in the figures), still (no parallax: images don't move on this site), **desaturated** (`grayscale(1) sepia(0.22) contrast(1.05)`, overridable via the `filtro` prop — the color comes from the brand scrim above, not from the photo), and always under a **scrim passed as `children`**: the scrim is tuned per section on where the text actually falls (full opacity over the text column, opening up over the figure; vertical instead of horizontal below `md`, where the columns stack). Readability comes first — worst case (a solid black photo under the light sections) still clears 4.5:1. Two stacking rules, both about the Viticcio: the host section must be `relative overflow-hidden` but must **not** create a stacking context (no `isolate`, no z-index), or the `z-10` figures stop sitting in front of the tendril and the weave is lost; and the content container must be `relative` and come *after* the background in the DOM, or the positioned background paints over the in-flow text. The tendril, which comes after the sections in the wrapper, correctly runs *over* these backgrounds.
- `src/components/Navbar.jsx` — fixed navbar that switches light/dark styling based on which section is under it; every section declares `data-nav-theme="dark"` or `"light"`. Any new section must set this attribute.
- `src/components/SanFlorian.jsx` — the most delicate component: a GSAP Flip transition zooms the bottle into a full-screen overlay (dialog) with tasting notes and a format selector. Scroll is locked via `document.body.style.overflow` while open. Below `lg` the zoomed bottle is fixed at the top and fades out as the overlay scrolls (`onOverlayScroll`) so it never covers the notes; its opacity is restored in `toggleZoom(false)` before the return Flip state is captured. While the overlay is active it sets `document.documentElement.dataset.sfZoom` (from open through close): the zoomed bottle goes `position: fixed`, leaves the flow and shortens the page, which would fire the Viticcio's ResizeObserver and rerun its (heavy) path regen on the main thread mid-Flip — the flag makes Viticcio skip that; `toggleZoom(false)`'s Flip `onComplete` clears the flag and dispatches `sf:relayout` so Viticcio recomputes once, settled. It also dispatches `sf:vola` (`{attivo}`) at both ends of the zoom: the bottle is one of the tendril's anchors, but the Flip lasts ~0.62 s while the overlay fades in 0.25 s, so for the rest of the flight the page is uncovered and the tendril would be drawn wrapped around the empty slot. It can't chase the bottle (nodes are measured, not tied to a transform), so it fades out for the crossing and comes back — the close event is fired *after* `sf:relayout`, i.e. bottle landed and paths re-measured.
- `src/components/Viticcio.jsx` — decorative tendril line (tortora, the logo color) drawn via `stroke-dashoffset` scrubbed with ScrollTrigger over all post-hero sections (they sit in a `relative` wrapper in `App.jsx`). The path is **generated at runtime**, not hand-authored: each breakpoint config lists nodes anchored to real page elements (fractions of an anchor's rect, `p(selector, fx, fy, {dy, asola?, ramo?, foglia?}, index?)`), joined by a **centripetal** Catmull-Rom spline that passes exactly through every node with smooth, fluid curves (no meander/ondulation — the line is deliberately restrained) — so the line adapts by itself to width changes and section-height variations (re-measured on `sf:ready`, window resize, and a ResizeObserver on the wrapper; all measures are skipped until `sf:ready` — before that the preloader covers everything and fonts/images still in flight would trigger wasted regens that steal main thread from the loading animation). The centripetal parameterization is load-bearing: the point list mixes waypoints hundreds of px apart with spiral samples a few px apart, and uniform Catmull-Rom kinks at those junctions. A node with `asola: {raggio, lato}` splices a sequence of **exact circular arcs** (`A` commands, not sampled points — the loop is geometrically perfect at every size) entered **tangent to the direction of travel** (estimated from the neighbouring nodes, or fixed via `direzione`): one full turn (half at full radius, half at 0.8) that re-emerges *inside* the ring already heading in the original travel direction, so the continuation crosses the ring exactly once at a sharp angle — a closed pencil-loop eyelet with no doubled tracts; the spline resumes from the arc's exit with its Bézier handle clamped to the arc's tangent (C1 throughout). The button wrap (`nodoAvvolgimento`) is the same standard eyelet, just anchored on the pill's right cap with a fixed entry direction. A node with `ramo: {lato, punti, figli?}` spawns a separate path that is a **simple bifurcation of the main spline**, not a parametric curve: it grafts tangent to the main line (same C1 saddling as the asole — the Bézier handle is clamped to the tangent) and then runs through its own `punti` array, joined by the exact same centripetal Catmull-Rom spline used for the main line. Each point in `punti` uses the **same page-anchored structure as the main line's nodes**: absolute page coordinates produced by `p(selector, fx, fy, …)` — a fraction of a real anchor's rect, typically the same anchor the branch's own graft node used — rather than the local stem-relative `{u, v}` offsets the branches used before; a branch tracks layout changes the way the main line does, instead of holding a fixed vector off its graft. Tuning a branch means editing those fractions, measured against the DOM like every other node, not an angle/length/radius formula (and not local `u`/`v` numbers either anymore — that scheme survives only for the small, fixed eyelet gesture, see `puntoLocale`/`PUNTI_CAPPIO`). A branch (at any level — top-level `ramo` or a `figli` sub-branch, since both go through the same `generaRamo`) can end in a `ricciolo`: `puntiRiccioloInduttore`, a row of self-intersecting loops (reusing the same eyelet construction as `asola`, factored into `costruisciCappio`) described one by one by a `spire` array, each `{ampiezza, spaziatura?, inclinazione?, lato?}` — `ampiezza` is that loop's radius, `spaziatura` is the straight-but-tangent run travelled *before* drawing it (default `0.9×ampiezza`, the ratio it shipped with before becoming configurable), `inclinazione` (degrees) rotates the tangent before that run so consecutive loops fan out or wave instead of sitting dead in line, and `lato` can flip winding direction loop to loop. It returns raw points appended to the stem's own point array *before* the single `splineSegmento` pass, so stem and curl are one continuous curve with no graft seam. (A second variant, `'piatta'` — a sampled shrinking spiral selected by `ricciolo.tipo` — existed and was removed; there is no `tipo` field any more.) Branches are deliberately **few but wide**: they live only where, diverging, they find open space or the outer edge of a photo to skirt — never parallel to the line, never hidden behind a `z-10` image, never crossing the line or another branch. `figli` lets a branch **fork**: each child grafts tangent to a point inside the parent's resolved point chain (index `da`, 0 = the graft itself) with the tangent estimated from its neighbours (same criterion as the main line), and has its own `punti`, recursively (`generaRamo` returns an array of paths). All forks are separate `subBranches` paths, so the main line stays single. (`catenaGambo`, the old integrated-curvature stem chain, survives only for the leaf twig — see `foglia` below — since it still needs a `svolta`-driven diverge-then-straight profile there.) Each path is revealed by the scrubbed timeline when the tip passes its graft point: the fraction of the main line at each graft is computed **at generation time** by a sampler that accumulates points and cumulative length while the path is emitted (pure arithmetic, rendered as `data-progresso`) — never by measuring the mounted path with `getPointAtLength`, which cost ~600 DOM calls ≈ 1.2 s of blocked main thread per regen (it froze the preloader mid-animation). The sampler feeds in travel order: spline tratta first, then the asola arcs (see the ordering comment in `generaViticcio` — swapping them double-counts lengths). A **sub-branch does not share its parent's fraction**: its graft sits on the parent, not on the main line, so asking the sampler for the nearest main-line point would give an arbitrary instant (in practice the parent's own, i.e. child and parent starting together, the child growing out of a stroke that hasn't reached it yet). It inherits the parent's main-line fraction and adds a `ritardo` — the cumulative length of the parent's polyline up to the graft index `da`, over the parent's total, times `DURATA_RAMO` — and the delays compound down the recursion, so a tertiary waits for the secondary that waits for the main line. Same rule for a leaf: its fraction is its twig's graft plus `DURATA_RAMO`, not the nearest main-line point to the leaf itself. Those two durations are constants shared between generation and the timeline precisely because the delay is computed in units of the other's draw time. The hidden state of every stroke (`nascondiTratto`) is `dasharray: l, l + 2m` with `offset: l + m`, `m = 3 × stroke width` — **both** dash boundaries a margin away from the path's two ends, and a gap longer than the dash so the pattern cannot repeat inside the path. Both halves are load-bearing, each fixes a different visible artifact: with a boundary exactly on the path start (`dasharray: l, offset: l`) Chrome still paints the round linecap of the dash that ends there — a dot sitting on the graft; and with the offset alone pushed past `l` (`dasharray: l`) the period `2l` repeats, the gap ends before the far end and the tail of the dash reappears — a detached stroke at the tip of every branch and a chunk of every leaf outline, floating until the line arrives. The margin scales with the stroke because the leaf lives in a scaled coordinate system where "3 px" is meaningless. Measuring here is treacherous: screenshot-diffing is useless (the scrub keeps easing between two shots, the main line moves a pixel and swamps the thing being measured — freeze `gsap.globalTimeline` and CSS animations first, and even then check two identical baseline shots), and rasterizing the paths with the same dash state into a canvas — the right tool for the wrap case, which it quantifies exactly (~35 px of ink per branch, 3–4.5 k per leaf) — does **not** reproduce the cap-on-the-boundary case, reporting 0 ink where the SVG shows the dot. Trust it only for what it models. The main line's start is the one **free end** of the whole drawing (branches begin on the line, leaves on the twigs), so its first pixels are a round cap parked in the middle of the hero long before they read as a line: the timeline therefore fades the line in over `EMERSIONE_LINEA` (3% of the draw) — measured: at 23 px drawn it is at opacity 0.12, fully opaque only at ~415 px, i.e. when it is unmistakably a line. Group `opacity` (not per-stroke alpha) keeps the grafts invisible. A node with `foglia: {lato, scala, rotazione}` (plus `altezza`/`lunghezza` from the config defaults) grows a **ramoscello**: the exact same stem logic as `ramo` (`catenaGambo`, tangent at the graft, diverging toward `lato`) but short and with no closing curl — it's rendered into `subBranches` alongside the real branches, same class/stroke/reveal treatment — and a vine-leaf outline (the wireframe traced in `references/leaf-wireframe.svg`, embedded verbatim as `FOGLIA_D`, a single stroke path with no fill) is grafted onto its tip. The leaf's petiole point (`FOGLIA_PICCIOLO`, measured — not the shape's bbox center) is anchored EXACTLY on the twig's tip, then rotated so its natural petiole→tip axis (`FOGLIA_ASSE`) continues the twig-tip's own tangent — the leaf carries the twig's motion straight through; `rotazione` adds per-leaf jitter and `scala` (× the config's `altezza` in px, and shared with the twig's own `lunghezza`) varies size — both exist purely so the handful of leaves don't read as one stamped shape. Same stroke weight and color as the tendril itself (`spessoreLinea`, `currentColor`) — stroke width is divided by the leaf's own `scala` before rendering (`spessoreLinea / f.scala`) because the SVG `scale()` used to size the leaf would otherwise scale the stroke too; `scala` is rounded to 3 decimals, not `arrotonda`'s 1 (its natural range is ~0.04–0.1, where 1-decimal rounding collapses every leaf to the same 0.1 and erases the size variation between them). Leaves are few (3 desktop, 2 mobile) and live only in the empty gaps BETWEEN sections, never on a node that already carries an `asola` or `ramo` — this is decoration on the seams, not a new density of detail along the line. Each leaf's outline is dash-revealed by the same scrubbed timeline as the branches, timed via the same generation-time fraction (rendered as `data-progresso` on its `<g>`, computed from the leaf's tip position). Placement was verified at 1024/1440/1920 and 360/390/430 px by a checker (scratch `collisioni.mjs`) that flags three things: line-over-text (`Range.getClientRects` boxes), branch fraction hidden inside image boxes, and branch-vs-branch / branch-vs-line proximity — all driven to zero. On mobile there is no room for the button wrap (Andrea's full-width bio sits between photo and CTA), so the tendril skips it and runs down the right margin — the CTA is `z-10` anyway. The **IntroVini band** is the one place where a section actively pushes the line around: its title is centred and sits exactly where the line used to run, so the crossing nodes anchor to `.vini-filo` (see IntroVini above) and the line detours right of the title on desktop, left of it on mobile, crossing the hairline — a decorative rule, the weave is welcome there. That detour forced two retunings that must stay together: the desktop bottle entry moved from `.sf-bottle -1.02, -0.199` to `-0.02` (the old node sat 18 px below the band, so coming back from the right channel in that gap was an elbow, not a curve), and the mobile leaf under Territorio moved from `fy 1.18` to `1.06` (the band swallowed the gap it lived in and left it straddling the band's edge). Anchor rects are measured via the `offsetTop`/`offsetLeft` chain (immune to the inline transforms GSAP reveals leave around; SVG anchors fall back to bounding-rect deltas minus GSAP's x/y). The line weaves through the layout: *behind* the elements marked `relative z-10` (Azienda figure, ChiSiamo CTA, Territorio figure, the San Florian bottle), *in front* of everything else (e.g. the second ChiSiamo photo), never touching text, ending on the ComingSoon drops logo. Two configs: desktop (≥ `lg`) and mobile (< `md`); in the 768–1023 band the tendril stays hidden (mixed layouts, no node set is safe). Node fractions were tuned against the real DOM: if a section's internal structure changes, retune by measuring, not by eye — but pure resizes now need no retuning. The "wrapping" illusion comes from asole that straddle the edges of `z-10` elements (half the ring hides behind the photo/bottle) and from `nodoAvvolgimento`, which coils around the rounded right cap of pill buttons — place such curls only where the element's visible contour coincides with its box edge, or the line seems to vanish into nothing. The scrub's range is **per config** (`corsa: {inizio, fine}`, default `top 75%` → `bottom bottom`), because it decides *where in the window the tip sits while it draws*: the line advances in path length while the page advances in pixels, and where the path descends faster than the scroll the tip runs below the fold — the reader then only ever sees line already drawn, never a line being drawn. Desktop is fine on the default (tip measured at 434–916 px in a 900 px window). Mobile is not: single column, ~6100 px of vertical drop over ~5000 px of usable scroll, and the tip sat at 870–1120 px in an 844 px window, i.e. below the fold for the *entire* run. Its range therefore ends later (`bottom 25%`), which stretches the draw over more scroll and lifts the tip back into the window (now ~480–800 px, and it lands on the ComingSoon logo with ~650 px of scroll to spare). Measure this with the tip's viewport y, not by watching: scratch `punta.mjs` walks the page in 300 px steps and prints where the tip is. Static (fully drawn) with `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Design system

- Brand palette is defined in `tailwind.config.js`: `antracite` #1D1D1B, `moro` #634E42, `tortora` #A48A7B, plus derived `creta`, `sabbia`, `offwhite`. Use these token names, not raw hex.
- Fonts: brand typefaces self-hosted from `public/fonts/` via `@font-face` in `src/index.css` (sources in `references/Fonts.zip`; two critical woff2 are preloaded in `index.html`). Mason Sans (`font-display`) for the wordmark and titles — it matches the bottle label lettering; LT Wave (`font-sans` and `font-prose`) for everything else. Mason Sans only ships 400/700: `font-medium`/`font-semibold` resolve to 400/700 via CSS font matching, LT Wave also has 300 (`font-light`). No Google Fonts.
- Shared component classes in `src/index.css`: `.eyebrow`, `.btn`, `.btn-dark`, `.btn-light`.
- **The logo is two SVGs and nothing else**: `DropsLogo.jsx` (the seven drops, traced from the client's PDF) plus `Wordmark.jsx` (the "Sette Fontane" lettering, vectorized from `references/sette-fontane-logo-completo.svg`). Navbar, hero and footer all use that same pair. Never re-set the name as live text to "look like" the logo — the footer did exactly that for a while (two lines of Mason Sans with hand-tuned tracking) and the mark ended up reading differently at the bottom of the page than at the top: the letterforms, weights and spacing of the real wordmark are not reproducible with a font. The visible mark is decorative; the accessible name comes from an adjacent `sr-only` span.
- Fluid typography via `clamp()` (e.g. `text-[clamp(2rem,5vw,4rem)]`), mobile-first layouts (`grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2`).
- Recurring formal motifs: round-arch images (`rounded-t-full`), the seven-drops logo rebuilt as SVG in `DropsLogo.jsx`, dark hero fading toward light backgrounds through tortora.
- Images: the figures are the *subjects*, the section backgrounds (`SfondoSezione`) are *depth* — large, quiet, always under a scrim. Territorio's panorama is now the background and its figure shows a detail (porfido terraces), so the same view isn't told twice. Images never move with the scroll: parallax was tried and removed on purpose — reveals are the only motion they get.

## GSAP pitfalls (learned the hard way)

- Inline transforms GSAP leaves on containers break `position: fixed` descendants — that's why `useReveal` uses `clearProps: 'transform'` on text reveals. Keep that in mind when animating anything that wraps a fixed element.
- Any tween that touches the San Florian bottle's transform (today: the format-change crossfade, a `gsap.from` that runs at mount and leaves an inline `translate(0,0)`; formerly also an idle bobbing tween, since removed) leaves inline transforms that override `.bottle-zoom`'s own `translate` — the zoomed bottle ends up half off-screen. That's why `toggleZoom(true)` does `clearProps: 'transform'` BEFORE capturing `Flip.getState`: keep it, and pause any looping transform tween there too (the glow pulse is paused/resumed this way).
- The bottle carries a huge `drop-shadow` (70px blur); recomputing it every frame while the Flip scales the bottle janks the whole zoom. The Flip therefore turns the filter off in `onStart` (`filter: 'none'`) and restores it in `onComplete` (`clearProps: 'filter'`) so only `transform` animates — keep that if you touch the zoom, and don't reintroduce a per-frame `filter`/`box-shadow` on an element GSAP scales.
- The bottle webp images in `public/img/` were cut out with a Python flood-fill from the jpgs in `references/`; those source jpgs have edge artifacts and a white-background reflection that must be removed if you regenerate them.
